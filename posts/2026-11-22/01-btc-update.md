# BTC bounces back above $60,000 after touching a two-year low

*2026-11-22 · post 1 of 3*

Bitcoin is trading around **$60,200** this morning (checked 22 November, ~09:51 Dhaka / ~03:51 UTC — sources: CoinStats, CoinGlass, Blockdeck), up roughly **2.2% over 24 hours**.

The bounce came off an overnight low near **$55,100** — BTC's weakest level since September 2024. The 24-hour range so far is about **$55,300–$60,800**, and the move reclaimed the $60,000 level lost on Friday. Market cap is back near **$1.21 trillion**.

What actually happened, according to today's reporting:

- **A $1.8 billion liquidation wave** flushed leveraged positions out over the weekend, then short covering pushed the price back up. The bounce looks mechanical — forced selling, then forced buying — rather than fresh demand arriving.
- **The bigger picture is still down.** BTC is down about **6% over 7 days** and **26% over 30 days**, and sits roughly **52% below its all-time high of $126,080**.
- **Sentiment is extreme fear.** The Fear & Greed Index read 11 on 21 November. Nobody is celebrating a 2% bounce after a month like this.

What I'm watching, not predicting:

1. **Whether $60,000 holds this time.** Yesterday it broke, today it's back — the level matters more as a line in the sand than as a price.
2. **Whether the bounce has follow-through.** A short-covering bounce that fades by Monday tells a different story than buyers actually stepping in.
3. **ETF flows next week.** Friday's redemptions were part of the selloff; if they keep coming, the bounce has no floor under it.

A green candle after a red month is not a trend change — it's a reminder that markets move both ways, fast. I check the price, write it down, and get back to building.
