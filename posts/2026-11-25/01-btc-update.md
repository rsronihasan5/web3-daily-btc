# BTC slips below $60,000 again — tariffs and strong US data hit risk appetite

*2026-11-25 · post 1 of 3*

Bitcoin is trading around **$59,500** this morning (checked 25 November, ~09:51 Dhaka / ~03:51 UTC — sources: Finnhub, Blockdeck, MetaMask price page), down roughly **3% over 24 hours** and about **8% over the last seven days**. Finnhub's Binance BTC/USDT quote put it at $59,478 at 03:46 UTC. Yesterday it was still holding near $61,000 — the $60,000 line gave way overnight.

The 24-hour range is about **$58,900–$60,800**. Market cap is near **$1.19 trillion**, with roughly **$29–30 billion** of BTC changing hands in the last day.

What's actually going on:

- **Tariffs and strong US data did the damage.** US tariff headlines landed in the evening, then November US consumer confidence came in at **103.3 — well above the 92.2 expected** — and BTC slid from about $60,390 to $59,199 in the early hours (BitKan Daily). Strong data is bad news for rate-cut hopes, and risk assets felt it.
- **Asian markets piled on.** After Asia opened this morning, South Korea's KOSPI fell about 5.3%, Taiwan stocks dropped about 3.7%, and chipmaker Advantest opened down 12.8%. BTC tested $59,180 again in the same move.
- **Fear is still extreme.** The Fear & Greed Index sits at **11–13 — Extreme Fear**. November is still down about **21%** from the 31 October close of $77,582, and BTC is roughly **53% below its all-time high of $126,080**.

What I'm watching, not predicting:

1. **The $59,000 area.** BTC has now tested the $59,180–$59,200 zone twice in a day. It keeps holding — for now.
2. **The $60,000 line.** It was support yesterday; now it's the first resistance to reclaim. Until BTC gets back above it, the tape stays heavy.
3. **Whether the weekend low near $55,130 comes into play.** A clean break below $59,000 reopens that level fast.
