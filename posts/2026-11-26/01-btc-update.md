# BTC bounces back above $60,000 — tariff scare fades, but fear is still extreme

*2026-11-26 · post 1 of 3*

Bitcoin is trading around **$60,700–$60,900** this morning (checked 26 November, ~09:51 Dhaka / ~03:51 UTC — sources: Finnhub, CoinGlass, Coinbase, Blockdeck), up roughly **+2.1–2.4% over 24 hours**. Yesterday it was sitting near $59,500 — the $60,000 line is back in BTC's hands, for now.

The 24-hour range is about **$57,300–$61,600**. Market cap is near **$1.22 trillion**, with roughly **$39–42 billion** of BTC changing hands in the last day. BTC is still down about **2–3% over the last seven days** and roughly **25% over the last 30 days**.

What's actually going on:

- **A dip below $58,000, then a bounce.** Hot core PCE data and fresh US tariff proposals knocked BTC under $58,000, and the Nasdaq 100 entered a bear market in the same move (news.bacoor.io). Buyers stepped in from there and BTC reclaimed $60,000 overnight.
- **ETF flows are still pointing the wrong way.** US spot BTC ETFs saw a net outflow of about **12,163 BTC (~$734.5 million) on 25 November** (IndustryLink). The bounce is happening despite institutions selling, not because of them.
- **Fear is still extreme.** The Fear & Greed Index sits at **11–13 — Extreme Fear**. A green day in an extreme-fear market is a relief bounce until proven otherwise.

What I'm watching, not predicting:

1. **The $61,600 area.** That's the top of the 24-hour range — a clean break above it would say the bounce has legs.
2. **The $59,000 area.** BTC tested $59,180–$59,200 twice yesterday and held. Losing it reopens the weekend low near $55,130.
3. **Whether ETF outflows slow down.** One day of outflows is noise; a week of them is a trend, and the trend is still red.

Green days feel good. They don't change the plan: check the price, write it down, keep building.
