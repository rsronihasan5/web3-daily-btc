# BTC holds above $60,000 for a second day — the rebound is real, the trend is not

*2026-11-23 · post 1 of 3*

Bitcoin is trading around **$60,900** this morning (checked 23 November, ~09:51 Dhaka / ~03:51 UTC — sources: CoinMarketCap, CoinGlass, Finnhub), up roughly **1.5–2% over 24 hours**.

This is the second straight day of gains after the weekend selloff drove BTC as low as **$55,130**. The 24-hour range is about **$58,700–$61,650**, and BTC is holding above the $60,000 line it lost on Friday. Market cap is near **$1.22 trillion**.

What actually happened, according to today's reporting:

- **The bounce is extending, not fading.** After Saturday's flush, short covering and dip-buying carried the price through Sunday and into Monday morning. Two green days in a row after a month of red is the first sign of stability in a while.
- **The weekly picture is still ugly.** BTC just closed its **7th straight weekly loss — the longest run since the Terra collapse**. It's down about **6% over 7 days**, **25% over 30 days**, and roughly **52% below its all-time high of $126,080**.
- **ETF flows are still the headwind.** US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw **$1.25 billion of net outflows for 16–20 November**, and November's outflows now total $3.59 billion with only two positive days all month. The bounce has no floor under it until that flow turns.

What I'm watching, not predicting:

1. **Whether $60,000 holds through the US session.** Holding it for a second day is good; holding it through a weekday is the real test.
2. **Whether buyers show up or it's just shorts covering.** Volume and ETF flows this week will tell the story.
3. **The $55,130 weekend low.** That's the line the market defended — if it breaks again, the bounce was just a pause.

Two green days don't erase a red month, but they do break the panic rhythm. I check the price, write it down, and get back to building.
