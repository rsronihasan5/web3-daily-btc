# BTC breaks below $60,000 — lowest since early July

*2026-11-21 · post 1 of 3*

Bitcoin is trading around **$58,900** this morning (checked 21 November, ~09:50 Dhaka / ~03:50 UTC — sources: CoinStats, Blockdeck, Finnhub), down roughly **5% over 24 hours** and about **14% over the past week**.

The $60,000 level is gone. BTC fell below it on Friday 20 November for the first time since early July, at one point dropping about $1,800 in roughly an hour. The 24-hour range so far is about **$57,800–$62,480**.

What's driving it, according to today's reporting:

- **A deepening US tech-stock selloff** dragging risk assets down with it — the Nasdaq was down over 5% on the day in the same window.
- **Record Bitcoin ETF redemptions**, adding sell pressure on top of the spot market.
- **Hawkish signals from the Federal Reserve**, which took the wind out of rate-cut hopes.

Zoom out and the slide is bigger than one bad day: BTC is down about **30% over the past 30 days** and roughly 33% year-to-date. Market cap is back near **$1.18 trillion**.

What I'm watching, not predicting:

1. **Whether $57,800 holds.** That's the low of the last 24 hours. Losing it opens the door to the mid-$50Ks that traders were already talking about yesterday.
2. **ETF flows next week.** If redemptions were a one-day panic or the start of a longer unwind matters more than any single candle.
3. **Whether the stock market stabilises.** This drop started in tech stocks, not in crypto — BTC is following, not leading.

Red days like this are when the "free mint" discipline matters most: no chasing, no leverage, no decisions made while the chart is still falling. I check the price, write it down, and get back to building.
